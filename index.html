Link Exchange
Finding partners, outreach, negotiation, and dashboard guide
Link exchange is an SEO off-page strategy where two websites agree to link to each other. The goal is building domain authority, organic traffic, and genuine industry relationships. Links placed within the body of an article carry more weight than footer or sidebar links.

2Quality criteria — what makes a good partner
Domain Rating
DR 50+ required
Check in Ahrefs. Anything below is not worth the time investment.
Monthly Traffic
5,000+ organic visits/month
Must be flat or growing — not declining. Declining traffic signals a site losing authority.
Page depth
Within 2 clicks from homepage
The closer to the homepage the better — deeper pages pass less link juice.
External links on the page
As few as possible
Pages with many outbound links dilute the value of each one. Fewer is stronger.
Semantic proximity
Topically aligned
The page linking to us must be about a related topic — not just any page on the site.
Content quality
High-quality, well-written
No PBNs, no spam directories, no thin content. Real editorial sites only.
Anchor text
Natural, relevant
Should align with the content of the page being linked. Avoid exact-match keyword stuffing.
Publication date
Recently published or updated
Fresh pages signal active maintenance. Very old pages may carry less crawl priority.

There are several ways to build your prospect list. No single method is enough on its own — combine 2–3 sources to get a meaningful batch. Every candidate still needs to pass the Ahrefs check (DR 50+, traffic 5,000+) before you invest time in finding the contact.
1Source 1 — Google SERP
Search your target keywords directly in Google and look at pages 1–3. Sites ranking here have proven traffic and topical relevance. You want blogs, media, guides, real estate portals, personal finance sites — not direct competitors.

Example queries: "comparateur offre électricité", "reducir factura luz hogar", "déménagement contrat énergie", "economizar internet casa".
2Source 2 — Ahrefs competitors backlinks
Take a competitor (Selectra, HelloWatt, Kelwatt) and look at who links to them that doesn't link to us yet. These sites are already proven link exchange candidates — they accept external links on relevant topics.

How: Ahrefs Site Explorer → paste competitor domain → Backlinks → filter: Dofollow only, One link per domain → export. Discard any domain that links to 10+ competitors (it's a link farm).
3Source 3 — Ahrefs Content Explorer
Find sites actively publishing content on your target topics. Go to Content Explorer → search with boolean operators, e.g. "énergie" AND "économiser" → filter: language, live pages only, exclude homepages and subdomains → sort by DR → export top results.
4Source 4 — AI ideation (ChatGPT / Gemini)
Useful for generating topic areas and site types you might not have thought of — not for finding specific domains (AI doesn't have live data).

Good prompt: "We're papernest, an energy and home contracts comparison platform. Give me 20 types of websites that would make natural link exchange partners — sites that target homeowners, renters, or people moving house, but are not direct energy comparators. Include site types, typical topics, and example formats."

Take the categories it gives you and then search those in Google or Ahrefs to find real domains.
Don't ask AI for specific domain names — it will hallucinate URLs that don't exist or misremember DR. Use AI for category generation, then verify real sites in Ahrefs.

6What to avoid
Hard no
PBNs and link farms
Links to 10+ competing sites, no real editorial content. The pattern looks manipulative to Google.
Hard no
Declining traffic
A site losing organic traffic has been penalised or is losing authority. A link from it is a liability.
Hard no
Spam directories
Hundreds of outbound links per page, no editorial value. No link juice passes.
Hard no
Irrelevant topics
A cooking blog linking to an energy comparator looks unnatural regardless of DR.
The goal is to find a specific person — ideally the marketing or content responsible — and ideally put 1–2 people from the same company in CC. Personal contacts have significantly higher reply rates than generic inboxes.
1Step by step — Hunter.io first
1
Search the domain in Hunter.io
Go to hunter.io, paste the domain, and look for people with roles like: Marketing Manager, Content Manager, SEO Manager, Head of Content, Digital Marketing. Hunter will show the email pattern used by the company and any verified addresses it has found.
2
Pick 1–2 contacts and put them in CC
If Hunter shows multiple marketing contacts, put the most relevant one in To and 1 other in CC. Sending to 2 people at the same company increases the chance that at least one sees it and forwards to the right person.
3
If Hunter has no individual — use the email pattern to guess
Hunter often shows the pattern used (e.g. firstname@domain.com) even without a specific person. Find the marketing responsible's name on LinkedIn, apply the pattern, verify with Verifalia before sending.
4
LinkedIn + Lusha as secondary option
If Hunter doesn't have anything useful: search [outlet name] + Marketing on LinkedIn, find the person, open Lusha extension on their profile to extract the email. This is slower but sometimes gets contacts that Hunter doesn't have.
5
Fallback — redaction, press, or generic email
Look on the site's Contact or About page for redaction@, press@, editorial@, or contact@. This triggers the generic sequence (ask to be redirected to the right person), not the direct pitch sequence.
6
Record everything in the Outreach tab
Fill: Name, Role, Email, Email method (Hunter / Pattern / Lusha / Generic), LinkedIn URL. The Email method column determines which email sequence to use.
The outreach sequence has one goal: booking a call. You're not trying to close a deal by email — you're trying to get 15 minutes on the phone with the right person. Below are the real templates used in YAMM campaigns.
1Two sequences depending on contact type
Personal email (marketing/content person)
Email 1: Direct pitch — introduce papernest, propose the exchange, ask for a call this week or next
Email 2 (day 6): Follow-up — reference the first email, restate the value briefly, ask for a quick call
Email 3 (day 10): Final follow-up — very short, one last ask
Generic email (info@ / redaction@)
Email 1: Ask to be redirected to the marketing responsible — don't pitch the exchange yet
Email 2 (day 6): Follow-up on the redirect request
Email 3 (day 10): Final follow-up — if still no response, mark Closed

3Sending with YAMM
1
Group contacts by campaign and batch in the Outreach tab
Campaign column: "New prospects / Re-contact / Gap vs. Competitors". Batch column: "Immo batch 1". This is what tracks the send in the dashboard.
2
Create the Gmail draft and send via YAMM
Draft in the assigned Gmail account → YAMM sheet → paste contacts + set campaign title. Follow-up date (day 6) is auto-calculated by Apps Script.
3
Update Status after each send
New → Contacted → Replied → Call booked → Deal / No reply / Closed. The History column logs each change automatically.
After the 3rd email with no reply, mark as "No reply" and stop. No 4th email — it damages deliverability and the relationship.
The call is a sales conversation — you need to sell the value of the exchange, not just describe it. Come prepared with their site's data and a clear proposal.
1Before the call
1
Review their site in Ahrefs
Know their DR, traffic, top pages. Identify 2–3 pages on their site where papernest could realistically be linked from — have the URLs ready.
2
Prepare the papernest pages you'll offer
Know the DR, traffic, and topic of the papernest page where their link will go. You'll need to show this as a KPI during the call — it's your side of the value proposition.
3
Prepare article topics to propose
Come with 3–4 topic ideas that would work for both sides. If they don't have a writer, offer to write the content — this removes their biggest friction point.
2On the call — what to cover
TopicWhat to say / do
Introduce the conceptWe help each other rank better by placing a contextual link in a relevant article on each site. Mutual benefit, no cost.
Show their pages you identifiedPropose specific URLs on their site where papernest could be mentioned. Show that you've done the homework.
Show papernest KPIsShare the DR and traffic of the page where their link will go. Make the value tangible.
Agree on scopeTarget 4 article collaborations — securing 3–4 links total. Agree on: number of links, anchor texts, content type, due dates.
Offer to writeIf they don't have a content team, offer to write the article for their site. This dramatically increases the deal conversion rate.
Review before publishBoth sides should review the content and live link before it's published. Agree on this upfront.
3After the call
Always send a follow-up email
Summarise what was agreed: the articles, the due dates, the anchor texts, the contact person. This becomes the reference document for the deal. Add the partner to the Active Deals tab in the dashboard.
The links to include in the articles are defined after the call — not before. Don't commit to specific URLs until you've agreed on the article topics.

4Live Links tab — completed exchanges
Once both links are live, move the deal here from Active Deals. This is your permanent record of live exchanges. Key fields to keep updated: Link to us live? and Link to them live? (YES/NO), Renewal date (some partners want to review the exchange after 6–12 months), Renewal status. The Dashboard auto-counts from this tab.
5ALL RD tab — full outreach history
Every domain ever contacted, regardless of outcome. Columns: Domain, Email used, Contact type (Marketing/Editorial/Generic), Owner, Market, Date contacted, Outcome (Replied / No reply / Closed), Campaign, Notes. This is your anti-spam check — before adding a domain to a new campaign, search here first to see if it was already contacted and what happened.
6Contacts Archive tab — reusable contacts
Contacts extracted from closed or inactive outreach who could be approached again in the future. Key column: Reusable? (YES / NO / Maybe). If YES — this person can be approached with a new campaign. If NO — the relationship ended badly or they explicitly asked not to be contacted again.
7Settings + Apps Script tabs
Settings: Maps owner names to email addresses (Romane → romane@papernest.com, Lauriane → laura.sanchez@papernest.com, etc.). Update this when the team changes — several auto-notifications depend on it.

Apps Script: The automation logic. It auto-calculates follow-up dates, logs status changes to the History column, and can trigger email alerts. To install or update: Extensions → Apps Script → paste the code from the Apps Script tab → save → reload the sheet → update owner emails in Settings.
